Physically, mentally, emotionally — through hard things, shared. We're not asking you to be consumed by it. We're asking for a commitment that lasts a little longer, and holds a little more authority over you, than your doubts do. That's where the change comes from.

What we're building

Some athletes get much better here. Some don't. After nine years, the difference is almost never talent and almost never luck. It's whether an athlete showed up to learn.

In school, you can chase the grade instead of the material and mostly get away with it. Most students do. School is built so that most people who put in the hours can earn a good grade, and a good grade feels like proof you learned something. If you could get the grade without learning, you'd take that deal.

Distance running doesn't offer that deal. You cannot get a good grade here without learning the material — it isn't possible. The sport is built so that achieving at a high level is hard for almost anyone. Some athletes spend four years looking for the shortcut and stay frustrated the whole time.

So the ask is simple, and it isn't small: show up to learn, with an open mind, and be willing to change. And understand that caring is action. What you do is what you believe, not what you say. When an athlete quietly slips out of a workout, that's an answer, even if nobody says anything out loud. If you're confused about why we're doing something, ask — that's always welcome. But silently opting out isn't neutral.

Built to peak in November

How we train

The plan is individual

Mileage and workouts vary dramatically from athlete to athlete. Nobody is trained like everybody.

We build in phases

Habits and skills first. Then fitness and mental strength. Then execution, when it counts.

Strength is daily

Strength and mobility work happens every day. It isn't optional and it isn't extra.

Recovery is training

The easy days are part of the plan, not a break from it. Peak fitness comes in November, not September.

What we ask

Of athletes

Show up to learn. Do the work when nobody is checking. Ask when you're confused — that's always welcome.

Of families

When you travel, help your athlete get their runs in. It doesn't have to take over the trip, but weeks off add up faster than almost anyone realizes, and a family trip shouldn't become the exit.
